Dysphagia & eosinophilic esophagitis

PRIMARY SOURCES: ACG Clinical Guideline: Eosinophilic Esophagitis (Dellon et al.) · AGREE consensus (2018) · Chicago Classification v4.0 (2021)

GI HandbookEsophagus · Dysphagia→

Bottom line

First split oropharyngeal (transfer difficulty, cough, nasal regurgitation — a swallow-study/ENT problem) from esophageal dysphagia (food sticks seconds after swallowing). For esophageal dysphagia, endoscopy is first-line, with biopsies even if the mucosa looks normal. Solid-food dysphagia in a younger adult with atopy and food impactions is EoE until biopsied; progressive dysphagia with weight loss is malignancy until excluded; and dysphagia to both solids and liquids from the start points to a motility disorder.

Escalate now
  • Food-bolus impaction with inability to handle secretions → urgent endoscopic disimpaction; never push a bolus blindly
  • Complete obstruction, drooling, or aspiration → airway and urgent endoscopy
  • Progressive solid-food dysphagia + weight loss + age >50 → exclude esophageal cancer promptly

Localize and scope

  • History usually localizes: transfer symptoms and airway signs → oropharyngeal (videofluoroscopic swallow, ENT/SLP). Retrosternal sticking → esophageal.
  • Esophageal dysphagia → EGD with ≥6 biopsies from at least two levels (proximal and distal) to evaluate for EoE, regardless of appearance.
  • If endoscopy is structurally normal and symptoms persist, high-resolution manometry evaluates for a motility disorder.

Eosinophilic esophagitis

  • Diagnosis: symptoms of esophageal dysfunction + ≥15 eosinophils/hpf on biopsy, after excluding other causes (EoE and GERD now overlap; PPI-responsiveness no longer excludes EoE).
  • Treatment — the "three Ds," used alone or combined: Drugs (PPI; swallowed topical steroids — budesonide oral suspension or fluticasone; dupilumab, FDA-approved for EoE in 2022), Diet (empiric elimination, commonly six- or step-up food elimination), and Dilation for fibrostenotic strictures.
  • Reassess histology after 8–12 weeks; EoE is chronic and relapses when therapy stops.

Motility disorders (when the scope is clean)

  • High-resolution manometry with the Chicago Classification v4.0 defines achalasia (types I–III), esophagogastric junction outflow obstruction, and spastic/hypercontractile disorders.
  • Achalasia is treated definitively with pneumatic dilation, laparoscopic Heller myotomy, or per-oral endoscopic myotomy (POEM); botulinum toxin is a bridge for poor operative candidates.
  • Always exclude pseudoachalasia (malignancy at the GEJ) in older patients with rapid weight loss — cross-sectional imaging or EUS.
Pearls for the note
  • Biopsy the "normal-looking" esophagus in dysphagia — EoE frequently hides behind subtle rings, furrows, or a normal exam.
  • After relieving a food impaction, arrange biopsies/follow-up; a large fraction of first impactions are undiagnosed EoE.
  • Dysphagia to liquids as much as solids from the outset is a motility flag, not a mechanical one.
  • New achalasia in an older patient with weight loss deserves a malignancy work-up before you call it primary.
