Acute severe ulcerative colitis — and the modern IBD map

PRIMARY SOURCES: ACG UC Guideline (Rubin et al., 2019) · AGA Guidelines, moderate-severe UC (2020) & CD (2021) · Truelove & Witts 1955

GI HandbookInflammatory Bowel Disease · Acute severe ulcerative colitis→

Bottom line

ASUC is a medical emergency with a built-in clock: IV methylprednisolone 60 mg/day, VTE prophylaxis (yes, even with bloody stools), rule out C. diff and CMV, and make the day-3 decision — steroid non-responders go to rescue infliximab or cyclosporine, with colorectal surgery already in the loop.

Escalate now
  • Toxic megacolon (transverse colon >6 cm + toxicity), peritonitis, or perforation → surgery, now
  • Day 3 on IV steroids: >8 stools/day, or 3–8 stools with CRP >45 mg/L (Oxford criteria) → rescue therapy or colectomy conversation
  • Deterioration at any point — falling albumin, rising HR, distension — don't wait for day 3

Recognize and stage: Truelove & Witts

ASUC = ≥6 bloody stools/day plus one marker of systemic toxicity: HR >90, temp >37.8 °C, Hgb <10.5 g/dL, or ESR/CRP elevation. Admit; this is not managed from clinic.

Day-1 bundle

  • IV methylprednisolone 60 mg/day (or hydrocortisone 100 mg q8h). More is not better.
  • Rule out mimics/aggravators: C. difficile on every admission (it doubles colectomy risk); flexible sigmoidoscopy (unprepped, minimal insufflation) with biopsies for CMV.
  • VTE prophylaxis: IBD flares are intensely prothrombotic — pharmacologic prophylaxis despite rectal bleeding is guideline-endorsed.
  • Stop the harmful: opioids, anticholinergics, loperamide, NSAIDs (megacolon risk). Hold oral 5-ASA during the acute phase.
  • Abdominal X-ray for baseline colonic diameter; feed the patient — bowel rest doesn't treat UC.
  • Check TPMT and hepatitis B status, TB screening early — you may need biologics within 72 h.

The day-3 decision

Apply the Oxford index on day 3 of IV steroids: >8 stools/day, or 3–8 stools/day plus CRP >45 mg/L predicts ~85% colectomy on steroids alone. Non-responders get rescue therapy:

Rescue therapy (CYSIF/comparative data: broadly similar efficacy)
OptionDosing logicChoose when
Infliximab5–10 mg/kg IV; accelerated dosing often used — hypoalbuminemia drains drug fastDefault at most centers; continues as maintenance
Cyclosporine2 mg/kg/day IV with level monitoringBridge to a different maintenance agent; prior anti-TNF failure

Colorectal surgery should meet the patient before rescue therapy fails — early colectomy is a life-saving operation, not a defeat, and delayed colectomy is where mortality lives.

Crohn's on the wards — the two-minute version

  • Penetrating/obstructive complications drive admissions: cross-sectional imaging (CTE/MRE) early; drain abscesses before starting or intensifying biologics.
  • Steroids induce, never maintain. Repeated steroid courses are a system failure — escalate to an advanced therapy plan.
  • Perianal fistulizing disease: exam under anesthesia + setons + anti-TNF is the classic combination.
  • Check the basics every admission: smoking cessation (the single best CD intervention), vaccinations, nutrition, and a documented IBD outpatient plan.
Pearls for the consult note
  • Write "day X of IV steroids" in every progress note — the whole ASUC pathway hangs on that counter.
  • A normal-looking CRP in a hypoalbuminemic, sick UC patient can be falsely reassuring; trend it with stool counts.
  • VTE prophylaxis in a bleeding colitis feels wrong and is right. Cite the guideline when questioned.
  • "C. diff negative ×1" on an ASUC admission isn't enough if suspicion is high — know your assay.
