Liver · Metabolic & alcohol

MASLD & alcohol-associated liver disease

PRIMARY SOURCES: AASLD Practice Guidance on MASLD (Rinella et al., 2023) · MASLD nomenclature consensus (2023) · ACG Guideline, Alcohol-Associated Liver Disease (Jophlin et al., 2024)

GI HandbookLiver & Cirrhosis · MASLD and MASH→

Bottom line

Metabolic dysfunction–associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD, the term that replaced NAFLD) is steatosis plus at least one cardiometabolic risk factor. Most patients never progress — so the job is risk-stratifying for fibrosis: FIB-4 first, then elastography. Treat the metabolic drivers (7–10% weight loss changes histology), and know that a distinct, dangerous entity — alcohol-associated hepatitis — is graded and treated on its own pathway.

Escalate now
  • Alcohol-associated hepatitis with Maddrey discriminant function ≥32 or MELD >20 → severe disease, high short-term mortality
  • Any decompensation (ascites, encephalopathy, variceal bleeding) → manage as decompensated cirrhosis; hepatology/transplant
  • Acute-on-chronic liver failure, hepatorenal AKI, or rising bilirubin/INR → escalate care

Define and stage MASLD

  • MASLD = hepatic steatosis + ≥1 cardiometabolic criterion (overweight/obesity, dysglycemia/T2DM, hypertension, or dyslipidemia). Coexisting significant alcohol use is termed MetALD.
  • Risk-stratify fibrosis noninvasively: FIB-4 is the first-line filter (low <1.3, indeterminate/high above), then elastography (VCTE/MRE) for the indeterminate or high group. Biopsy is reserved for uncertainty.
  • Fibrosis stage — not the presence of steatosis — is the strongest predictor of liver-related and overall mortality.

Treat the drivers

  • Weight loss is foundational: ~5% reduces steatosis, ~7–10% improves steatohepatitis and can regress fibrosis. Mediterranean-style diet, activity, and structured programs.
  • Optimize cardiometabolic risk aggressively — cardiovascular disease, not liver disease, is the leading cause of death in MASLD. Statins are safe and indicated when otherwise warranted.
  • Two drugs are now FDA-approved for non-cirrhotic MASH with F2–F3 fibrosis: resmetirom (a thyroid hormone receptor-β agonist, 2024) and semaglutide 2.4 mg weekly (2025, on the ESSENCE trial). Other GLP-1 receptor agonists and tirzepatide help through weight loss; consider bariatric therapy in eligible patients with obesity.

Alcohol-associated liver disease

  • Abstinence is the single most important intervention across the spectrum; treat alcohol use disorder concurrently.
  • Alcohol-associated hepatitis: rapid-onset jaundice, AST:ALT typically >1.5–2 (values usually <300–400), tender hepatomegaly. Grade severity with MELD (>20 = severe, the ACG 2024 preference) or the Maddrey discriminant function (≥32).
  • Severe disease without contraindication: consider prednisolone 40 mg daily; reassess response with the Lille score at day 7 (day 4 is acceptable) and stop steroids in non-responders. Nutrition is essential; watch for infection and hepatorenal syndrome.
  • Early liver transplantation for selected non-responders is an accepted option at experienced centers.
Pearls for the note
  • Put a FIB-4 in your assessment of any fatty liver — it's the cheapest way to separate the many who need reassurance from the few who need hepatology.
  • Don't withhold a statin from MASLD out of fear; these patients die of cardiovascular disease.
  • AST > ALT with AST usually under ~400 in a jaundiced drinker is alcohol-associated hepatitis, not a random transaminitis — grade it.
  • "Fatty liver" on ultrasound is a starting point, not a diagnosis — stage the fibrosis and name the metabolic drivers.

Trial / studyYearOne-line verdict
