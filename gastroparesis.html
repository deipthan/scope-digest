Gastroparesis & chronic nausea

PRIMARY SOURCE: ACG Clinical Guideline: Gastroparesis (Camilleri et al., 2022)

GI HandbookStomach & H. pylori · Gastroparesis→

Bottom line

Gastroparesis is delayed gastric emptying without mechanical obstruction, plus symptoms (nausea, vomiting, early satiety, bloating, postprandial fullness). Confirm it with a 4-hour solid-phase gastric emptying scintigraphy — but only after excluding obstruction and stopping medications that slow the gut (opioids, GLP-1 receptor agonists, anticholinergics) for the test. Most cases are diabetic, idiopathic, or post-surgical, and management is diet plus judicious prokinetics.

Escalate / reconsider the diagnosis
  • Dehydration, electrolyte derangement, or inability to maintain nutrition/hydration → admit for repletion
  • Mechanical obstruction or malignancy not yet excluded → image and scope before labeling it gastroparesis
  • Cyclic vomiting relieved by hot showers / heavy cannabis use → cannabinoid hyperemesis, a different problem with a different fix (cessation)

Confirm the diagnosis correctly

  • Exclude mechanical obstruction and mucosal disease first (EGD ± imaging).
  • Stop emptying-slowing drugs before testing — opioids, GLP-1 and amylin analogues, anticholinergics, and cannabinoids all cause false-positive delayed emptying.
  • Gold standard: 4-hour solid-phase scintigraphy (the 90-minute study misses cases); retained >10% at 4 hours is abnormal. Wireless motility capsule or breath testing are alternatives.
  • Optimize glycemic control — acute hyperglycemia itself slows emptying and confounds the test.

Diet and glycemic foundation

  • Small, frequent meals; low-fat and low-insoluble-fiber; more liquid-based calories when solids aren't tolerated.
  • Correct dehydration and electrolytes; address nutrition early, escalating to enteral (post-pyloric/jejunal) feeding if oral intake fails. Parenteral nutrition is a last resort.
  • Tighten diabetes control — it both drives and worsens the condition.

Pharmacologic and procedural therapy

  • Metoclopramide is the first-line prokinetic — lowest effective dose, shortest duration, with a black-box warning for tardive dyskinesia (liquid formulation aids titration). Domperidone (access-limited) and, in refractory cases, other agents are alternatives.
  • Antiemetics control symptoms but do not speed emptying; use them adjunctively.
  • Refractory disease: consider pyloric-directed therapy (endoscopic G-POEM/pyloromyotomy, botulinum toxin has limited evidence) or gastric electrical stimulation in selected centers.
  • Antidepressant "neuromodulators" may help symptom burden in functional overlap but do not correct emptying.
Pearls for the note
  • Before you order the emptying study, list the drugs you stopped — an opioid or GLP-1 agonist on board invalidates it.
  • The 90-minute emptying scan is not adequate; insist on the 4-hour protocol.
  • GLP-1 receptor agonists are an increasingly common, reversible cause of gastroparesis-like symptoms — ask about them explicitly.
  • Symptom severity correlates poorly with the degree of delay — treat the patient, not just the scintigraphy number.
