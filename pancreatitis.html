Acute pancreatitis

PRIMARY SOURCES: ACG Guideline, Acute Pancreatitis (Tenner et al., 2024 update) · WATERFALL NEJM 2022 · Revised Atlanta 2012

GI HandbookPancreas & Biliary · Acute pancreatitis→

Bottom line

Diagnose with 2 of 3 (pain, lipase >3× ULN, imaging) — you usually don't need the CT on day 1. Resuscitate with moderate, not aggressive, lactated Ringer's; feed a low-fat oral diet within 24–48 hours as tolerated; give no prophylactic antibiotics; reserve urgent ERCP for cholangitis; and take the mild gallstone pancreatitis to cholecystectomy this admission.

Escalate now
  • Concurrent cholangitis (fever + jaundice + biliary obstruction) → ERCP within 24 h
  • Persistent organ failure >48 h (severe AP by Revised Atlanta) → ICU-level care
  • BISAP ≥3, rising BUN/Hct despite fluids, or new hypoxia — the trajectory is the alarm

Diagnose — and hunt the cause

  • 2 of 3: characteristic epigastric pain, lipase/amylase >3× ULN, imaging findings. If the first two are present, early CT adds little and can be deferred; reserve it for diagnostic doubt or failure to improve at 48–72 h.
  • Every patient gets a right upper quadrant ultrasound (gallstones), triglycerides (>1000 implicates hypertriglyceridemia), and calcium. Review alcohol, medications, recent ERCP.
  • Idiopathic after a real workup? That's a GI referral (EUS/MRCP), especially after a second episode or age >40 — occult malignancy deserves a thought.

Fluids: moderate won the argument

WATERFALL (NEJM 2022) randomized aggressive (20 mL/kg bolus → 3 mL/kg/h) versus moderate resuscitation (10 mL/kg bolus if hypovolemic → 1.5 mL/kg/h) and was stopped early: aggressive fluids caused more volume overload without improving outcomes. Use lactated Ringer's, reassess at 6–12 h intervals (BUN, Hct, urine output), and de-escalate deliberately.

Feed early, feed by mouth

  • Mild AP: low-fat oral diet within 24–48 h as pain and nausea allow — no clear-liquid purgatory, no waiting for lipase to normalize.
  • Can't tolerate PO by ~72 h: enteral tube feeding (NG is acceptable; jejunal if not tolerated). Enteral beats parenteral — it protects the gut barrier and reduces infectious complications.
  • Pain control: multimodal; opioids as needed without dogma.

What not to give, and when ERCP actually helps

  • No prophylactic antibiotics, even for predicted severe or necrotizing disease. Antibiotics are for documented infection — infected necrosis (suspect with gas on CT or deterioration ≥2–4 weeks in) or extra-pancreatic infection.
  • ERCP is not a pancreatitis treatment. It's for pancreatitis with cholangitis (within 24 h) or persistent biliary obstruction. Gallstone pancreatitis without either does not benefit from urgent ERCP.
  • Infected necrosis: antibiotics that penetrate necrosis (e.g., carbapenem) and delay intervention where possible; step-up approach (percutaneous → endoscopic necrosectomy) beats open surgery.

Before discharge: close the loop

  • Mild gallstone pancreatitis → same-admission cholecystectomy. PONCHO (Lancet 2015): interval surgery meant more recurrent biliary events. Waiting "a few weeks" is a readmission plan.
  • Necrotizing gallstone disease: delay cholecystectomy until inflammation settles.
  • Alcohol etiology: brief intervention and referral — it measurably reduces recurrence.
  • Hypertriglyceridemia: fibrate on discharge, insulin/apheresis acutely only for severe cases with ongoing symptoms.
Pearls for the consult note
  • Lipase height does not grade severity — trajectory, BUN, and organ failure do. Use BISAP at the door.
  • "Day-1 CT to confirm pancreatitis" in a classic presentation is the most commonly wasted scan in the hospital.
  • Document Revised Atlanta severity (mild / moderately severe / severe) — it standardizes your note and your escalation logic.
  • The discharge line that prevents the bounce-back: "surgery consulted for same-admission cholecystectomy per PONCHO."

Landmark evidence

Trial / studyYearOne-line verdict
WATERFALL (NEJM)2022Aggressive fluids caused harm (volume overload) without benefit — moderate LR is the standard.
PONCHO (Lancet)2015Same-admission cholecystectomy for mild gallstone AP prevents recurrent biliary events.
PYTHON (NEJM)2014Early nasoenteric feeding showed no advantage over on-demand oral feeding at 72 h.
PANTER (NEJM)2010Step-up approach for infected necrosis beat open necrosectomy.
