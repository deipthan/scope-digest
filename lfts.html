Abnormal liver chemistries

PRIMARY SOURCE: ACG Clinical Guideline: Evaluation of Abnormal Liver Chemistries (Kwo et al., 2017)

GI HandbookLiver & Cirrhosis · Abnormal liver chemistries→

Bottom line

Before ordering a shotgun panel, name the pattern: hepatocellular (ALT/AST up), cholestatic (ALP ± bilirubin up), or mixed — the R-factor makes this quantitative. The pattern chooses the work-up. Also remember the true upper limit of normal for ALT is lower than most lab reports suggest (~29–33 U/L in men, ~19–25 in women), so "mildly elevated" is often real.

Escalate now
  • Aminotransferases in the thousands → ischemic hepatitis, acute viral, acetaminophen/toxin, or autoimmune — treat the cause urgently and check acetaminophen level
  • Jaundice + coagulopathy (INR ≥1.5) + any encephalopathy in a patient without known cirrhosis → acute liver failure, transplant-center call
  • Cholestasis with fever and RUQ pain → cholangitis; don't sit on it

Classify the pattern

  • Hepatocellular: ALT/AST-predominant. Cholestatic: ALP-predominant (confirm hepatic origin with GGT or fractionated ALP). Mixed: both.
  • The R-factor = (ALT/ULN) ÷ (ALP/ULN): >5 hepatocellular, <2 cholestatic, 2–5 mixed.
  • AST:ALT >2 (with both modestly elevated) suggests alcohol-associated liver injury; an isolated AST rise can be muscle (check CK) or hemolysis.

Work up by pattern

Targeted evaluation
PatternFirst-line evaluation
HepatocellularViral hepatitis serologies (HBV, HCV, ± HAV/HEV), history of alcohol and medications/supplements, metabolic risk (MASLD), ferritin/transferrin saturation (hemochromatosis), and — especially in the young — autoimmune markers (ANA, ASMA, IgG), ceruloplasmin (Wilson), and A1AT. Consider celiac and thyroid.
CholestaticRUQ ultrasound first. Ducts dilated → obstruction (stones, stricture, mass) → MRCP/EUS/ERCP. Ducts normal → AMA (primary biliary cholangitis), MRCP (primary sclerosing cholangitis), drug-induced cholestasis, infiltrative disease.

Common traps and next steps

  • Always take a rigorous medication and supplement history — drug-induced liver injury (including herbals and anabolic steroids) is a leading cause and a diagnosis of exclusion.
  • Mild, stable, asymptomatic elevations with a bland work-up and metabolic risk are usually MASLD — but confirm with imaging and fibrosis staging (FIB-4), don't just assume.
  • Persistent unexplained elevations after a first-pass work-up warrant hepatology referral and, in selected cases, biopsy.
Pearls for the note
  • State the pattern and R-factor in your assessment — it turns a vague "elevated LFTs" into a directed plan.
  • In anyone <40 with unexplained hepatitis, check ceruloplasmin — missed Wilson disease is a catastrophe you can prevent.
  • Isolated hyperbilirubinemia that is unconjugated with normal enzymes and hemolysis excluded is usually Gilbert syndrome — reassure, don't over-image.
  • An elevated ALP is not automatically liver — fractionate or check GGT to confirm hepatic origin before chasing the biliary tree.
