Diverticulitis

PRIMARY SOURCES: ACG Clinical Guideline: Diverticular Disease (Peery et al., 2021) · AGA Guideline (Stollman et al., 2015)

GI HandbookDiverticulitis · the full chapter→

Bottom line

CT confirms it and — more importantly — sorts uncomplicated from complicated. In the immunocompetent patient with mild, uncomplicated diverticulitis, antibiotics can be used selectively rather than reflexively. Complicated disease (abscess, perforation, obstruction, fistula) needs antibiotics plus drainage or surgery. After a first episode, colonoscopy in 6–8 weeks excludes an underlying cancer, and elective surgery is individualized — not dictated by counting episodes.

Escalate now
  • Diffuse peritonitis, free perforation, or sepsis → resuscitate, broad-spectrum antibiotics, urgent surgery
  • Abscess >3–4 cm → image-guided percutaneous drainage
  • Large-bowel obstruction or colovesical fistula (pneumaturia, fecaluria) → surgical planning
  • Immunocompromise → lower threshold for admission, antibiotics, and intervention

Diagnose and stratify

  • CT abdomen/pelvis with contrast is the test of choice — it confirms the diagnosis and identifies abscess, perforation, obstruction, or fistula.
  • Uncomplicated = inflammation without those features; complicated = any of them.
  • Clinical severity (fever, leukocytosis, peritoneal signs, comorbidity, immune status) drives inpatient vs outpatient management as much as the CT.

Treat by category

Management by severity
CategoryApproach
Mild, uncomplicated, immunocompetent, reliable outpatientConsider observation without antibiotics; oral intake as tolerated, close follow-up
Uncomplicated but systemically unwell / comorbid / immunocompromisedAntibiotics (cover gram-negatives + anaerobes), bowel rest as needed, admit if unable to tolerate PO
AbscessAntibiotics + percutaneous drainage if >3–4 cm; small abscesses may resolve on antibiotics
Perforation / peritonitis / obstructionResuscitation, broad-spectrum antibiotics, surgery (resection ± diversion)

After the acute episode

  • Colonoscopy 6–8 weeks after resolution of a first episode (or complicated disease) to exclude malignancy and confirm diverticular disease — unless a recent quality colonoscopy already did so.
  • Elective sigmoid resection is decided by the impact on the individual (frequency, severity, complications, quality of life, fistula/stricture), not a fixed episode threshold.
  • Long-term prevention: a high-fiber diet is reasonable; the old "avoid nuts and seeds" advice is not evidence-based. Mesalamine and rifaximin are not recommended to prevent recurrence.
Pearls for the note
  • "Uncomplicated diverticulitis, immunocompetent, mild" is now a scenario where you can justify withholding antibiotics — document the reasoning and the follow-up plan.
  • Don't skip the interval colonoscopy after a first episode — the point is to not miss a perforated colon cancer masquerading as diverticulitis.
  • Nuts, seeds, and popcorn do not need to be avoided; that advice has been overturned.
  • Recurrent left-lower-quadrant pain in a young patient with fistula symptoms should raise Crohn's as an alternative diagnosis.

Landmark evidence
