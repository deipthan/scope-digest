Endoscopy for the non-endoscopist

PRIMARY SOURCES: ACG/CAG Guideline, antithrombotics in acute bleeding & periendoscopic period (Abraham et al., 2022) · ASGE guidance on sedation, prophylaxis & procedural risk

Bottom line

The best GI consult is a decision-ready one: know the question, the urgency, the NPO status, the anticoagulants, and the goals of care before you page. For elective procedures, most antithrombotic decisions reduce to: continue aspirin, don't bridge warfarin for most patients, and hold DOACs briefly for high-risk procedures.

Page now, not at sign-out
  • Food bolus with inability to handle secretions, or any esophageal button battery — emergent
  • Suspected variceal bleed, unstable UGIB, cholangitis needing decompression
  • Caustic ingestion, or a sharp/long object proximal to the duodenum

What the fellow needs from you (the perfect page)

  • The question: "EGD for hemostasis," not "GI bleed, please evaluate."
  • Stability & airway: vitals trajectory, transfusions given, pressors, mental status.
  • Anticoagulation inventory: agent, last dose, indication, renal function.
  • NPO time, code status/goals, and consent capacity (or the surrogate's phone number).
  • Relevant anatomy: prior surgeries (Roux-en-Y changes everything), prior scopes and findings.

Antithrombotics, elective setting (ACG/CAG 2022)

  • Aspirin for secondary prevention: continue for essentially all endoscopy, including polypectomy.
  • Warfarin: continue for low-risk procedures; for high-risk procedures hold ~5 days — and do not bridge for most patients (BRIDGE, NEJM 2015: bridging in AF gave more bleeding, no fewer strokes). Bridging is reserved for the highest thrombotic risk (e.g., mechanical mitral valve, recent VTE/stroke) — involve cardiology.
  • DOACs: hold ~1–2 days before high-risk procedures (longer with renal impairment for dabigatran); no bridging; resume when hemostasis is secure, typically within 1–3 days.
  • P2Y12 inhibitors (dual antiplatelet): for high-risk procedures, hold the P2Y12 (~5–7 days) while continuing aspirin; coordinate with cardiology if a recent stent — elective procedures are usually deferred within 6–12 months of drug-eluting stents.
  • In acute bleeding: hold the agent; reversal only for life-threatening hemorrhage; resume early once hemostasis achieved — restarting anticoagulation lowers mortality.

Sedation, NPO, and prophylaxis basics

  • NPO: typically ~2 h clear liquids, 6–8 h solids; unplanned urgent scopes weigh aspiration risk against bleeding tempo — that's part of the GI/anesthesia conversation.
  • ASA class, OSA, and a difficult airway history determine anesthesia support vs moderate sedation.
  • Antibiotic prophylaxis is not routine for endoscopy. Established uses: PEG placement, cirrhosis with GI bleeding, ERCP with anticipated incomplete drainage (e.g., PSC, hilar strictures), and cyst drainage.
  • Bowel prep quality is destiny for inpatient colonoscopy — split-dose PEG, verified by an actual look at the output, prevents the "repeat scope" note.

After the procedure: what to watch on the floor

  • Post-polypectomy syndrome (pain, fever, leukocytosis with negative imaging) vs perforation — CT decides.
  • Post-ERCP pancreatitis (~5–10% overall): new epigastric pain + lipase; rectal indomethacin should already have been given in high-risk cases.
  • Delayed post-polypectomy bleeding can occur up to ~2 weeks out — relevant for anticoagulation resumption planning.
  • Document the resumption date for every held antithrombotic in the discharge summary. This is the most litigated blank space in medicine.
Pearls for the consult note
  • "Continue aspirin" is the default answer, and knowing that marks you as GI-literate.
  • The BRIDGE trial is the citation for "no heparin bridge" pushback — one line, ends the argument.
  • An INR of 1.8 rarely delays a needed diagnostic EGD in bleeding; ask before empirically transfusing FFP.
  • Before consenting for colonoscopy, ask what happens if they find a big polyp — the anticoagulation plan may hinge on it.

Landmark evidence

Trial / studyYearOne-line verdict
BRIDGE (NEJM)2015Heparin bridging of warfarin in atrial fibrillation: more bleeding, no fewer strokes.
PAUSE (JAMA Intern Med)2019A standardized 1–2 day DOAC interruption with no bridging and no drug levels gave <2% major bleeding and <1% thromboembolism.
Sung (Ann Intern Med)2010Continuing aspirin after an ulcer bleed in cardiovascular patients lowered mortality despite more rebleeding.
