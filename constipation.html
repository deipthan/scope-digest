Chronic constipation & OIC

PRIMARY SOURCES: ACG Clinical Guideline: Management of Chronic Constipation (2023) · AGA/ACG guidance on opioid-induced constipation · Rome IV criteria

GI HandbookConstipation & Anorectal Disorders · Chronic idiopathic constipation→

Bottom line

Most chronic constipation is managed empirically: rule out alarm features and secondary causes, then build up from fiber and osmotic laxatives (PEG) to secretagogues and prokinetics as needed. The step residents miss is dyssynergic defecation — a pelvic-floor problem that laxatives won't fix and that anorectal manometry plus biofeedback will. Opioid-induced constipation gets a mechanism-matched drug: a PAMORA.

Investigate / escalate
  • Alarm features — hematochezia, iron-deficiency anemia, unintentional weight loss, new constipation after age 50, or family history of colorectal cancer/IBD → colonoscopy
  • Acute colonic pseudo-obstruction (Ogilvie) with marked distension and cecal diameter >12 cm → risk of perforation; decompress (neostigmine if no contraindication)
  • Obstipation with vomiting and distension → exclude mechanical obstruction

Confirm it's primary constipation

  • Apply Rome IV criteria and exclude secondary causes: medications (opioids, anticholinergics, calcium-channel blockers, iron), hypothyroidism, hypercalcemia, diabetes, neurologic disease.
  • Check alarm features; do a digital rectal exam — it screens for dyssynergia (paradoxical contraction, poor push effort, anismus) and is routinely skipped.
  • Colonoscopy is for alarm features or age-appropriate screening, not for uncomplicated constipation alone.

Step up therapy

Escalation for chronic idiopathic constipation
TierOptions
FirstSoluble fiber (psyllium) + adequate fluid; PEG (polyethylene glycol) osmotic laxative
SecondStimulant laxatives (bisacodyl, senna) as needed
Third (prescription)Secretagogues: linaclotide, plecanatide, lubiprostone; prokinetic: prucalopride (5-HT4 agonist)
  • Fiber can worsen bloating in slow-transit or dyssynergic constipation — escalate thoughtfully rather than pushing more fiber into a non-responder.

Don't miss the two special cases

  • Dyssynergic defecation: suspect it when laxatives fail or the exam suggests outlet dysfunction. Confirm with anorectal manometry + balloon expulsion (± defecography). Treatment is pelvic-floor biofeedback, which outperforms laxatives for this phenotype.
  • Opioid-induced constipation (OIC): if opioids can't be reduced, use a peripherally acting mu-opioid receptor antagonist (PAMORA) — naloxegol, naldemedine, or subcutaneous methylnaltrexone — which relieve constipation without reversing analgesia. Start a prophylactic bowel regimen whenever opioids begin.
Pearls for the note
  • Do the rectal exam and document push effort and sphincter behavior — it's the cheapest test for the diagnosis laxatives can't treat.
  • "Failed fiber and laxatives" is the trigger to test for dyssynergia, not to keep adding laxatives.
  • Start a bowel regimen the moment you start an opioid; treat established OIC with a PAMORA, not just more senna.
  • New constipation after 50, or with any alarm feature, is a colonoscopy — not an empiric-laxatives — story.
