Decompensated cirrhosis: the admission playbook

PRIMARY SOURCES: AASLD Guidance — Ascites/SBP/HRS (Biggins et al., 2021) · Hepatic Encephalopathy (2014) · CONFIRM NEJM 2021

GI HandbookLiver & Cirrhosis · ascites, SBP, HRS, encephalopathy→

Bottom line

Every cirrhotic admitted with ascites gets a diagnostic paracentesis within 24 hours — SBP is silent and untreated mortality is brutal. Treat SBP with a third-generation cephalosporin plus albumin on days 1 and 3. Treat encephalopathy by finding the precipitant, not just ordering lactulose. And call AKI in cirrhosis early: HRS-AKI now has an FDA-approved drug (terlipressin).

Escalate now
  • PMN ≥250/mm³ on the tap → treat immediately; don't wait for culture
  • AKI not responding to 48 h of volume expansion → HRS-AKI pathway, hepatology involvement
  • Grade III–IV encephalopathy → airway assessment, ICU consideration
  • Any decompensation with MELD rising: is this patient at a transplant center, and if not, why not?

Tap every belly

  • Diagnostic paracentesis on every admission with ascites, regardless of symptoms. Send cell count with differential, culture in blood-culture bottles at the bedside, albumin, and total protein.
  • Coagulopathy is not a contraindication — INR and platelets don't predict bleeding from a para; don't transfuse prophylactically.
  • SAAG ≥1.1 g/dL confirms portal hypertension as the driver.

SBP: cephalosporin + albumin, then lifelong prophylaxis

  • PMN ≥250/mm³ = SBP. Treat with cefotaxime or ceftriaxone 2 g IV daily for 5–7 days; escalate coverage for nosocomial episodes or prior resistant organisms.
  • Albumin 1.5 g/kg on day 1, 1 g/kg on day 3 — reduces renal failure and mortality (Sort, NEJM 1999).
  • After the first episode: secondary prophylaxis for life (norfloxacin where available, or ciprofloxacin/TMP-SMX).
  • Nonselective beta-blockers: reassess during SBP with hypotension or AKI.

Ascites management beyond the tap

  • Sodium restriction (2 g/day) plus spironolactone-based diuresis, typically spironolactone:furosemide in a 100:40 ratio, titrated.
  • Large-volume paracentesis (>5 L removed): give albumin 6–8 g per liter removed.
  • Refractory ascites: serial LVP vs TIPS evaluation; stop NSAIDs forever; reassess NSBB dosing during hemodynamic stress.

Hepatic encephalopathy: the precipitant is the diagnosis

  • Hunt the trigger: infection (tap them), GI bleeding, constipation, dehydration/AKI, sedatives, TIPS, nonadherence. Ammonia levels don't grade severity and rarely change management in the classic presentation.
  • Lactulose titrated to 2–3 soft stools/day; add rifaximin 550 mg BID for secondary prophylaxis after a second episode (Bass, NEJM 2010).
  • Do not protein-restrict — sarcopenia kills cirrhotics; target 1.2–1.5 g/kg/day protein.

AKI and hepatorenal syndrome — the modern pathway

  • Stage AKI, stop diuretics and nephrotoxins, and give albumin 1 g/kg/day × 2 days as a volume challenge.
  • No response + criteria met (no shock, no nephrotoxins, bland sediment, no structural disease) = HRS-AKI.
  • Terlipressin + albumin (CONFIRM, NEJM 2021; FDA-approved 2022) — watch for pulmonary edema and ischemia; caution with grade III ACLF or hypoxia. Norepinephrine in the ICU is the alternative; midodrine + octreotide is the floor-level fallback and the weakest option.
  • HRS-AKI is a transplant conversation, not just a nephrology consult.

Before discharge

  • Recalculate MELD 3.0; ≥15 (or any decompensation) → transplant referral discussion.
  • Confirm HCC surveillance is scheduled (ultrasound ± AFP every 6 months).
  • Medication reconciliation: no NSAIDs, careful with PPIs without an indication, verify SBP prophylaxis and rifaximin made it onto the list.
Pearls for the consult note
  • "Admitted with ascites, para deferred" is the phrase most associated with missed SBP — do the tap the day of admission.
  • Albumin has exactly three evidence-backed uses here: SBP (days 1 & 3), LVP >5 L, and the HRS volume challenge. Cite them; push back on the rest.
  • An encephalopathic cirrhotic with "normal" vitals still gets an infection workup including paracentesis.
  • Document Child-Pugh and MELD 3.0 in every H&P — they anchor prognosis conversations and TIPS/transplant logic.
