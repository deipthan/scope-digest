Colorectal cancer screening & surveillance

PRIMARY SOURCES: USPSTF 2021 · US Multi-Society Task Force (post-polypectomy 2020; family history & quality documents) · ACG 2021

GI HandbookPolyps & Colorectal Cancer · Screening→

Bottom line

Average-risk screening starts at 45. Colonoscopy every 10 years and annual FIT are the workhorses; the best test is the one that gets done. Family history moves the start to age 40 or 10 years before the youngest affected relative, with 5-yearly colonoscopy if a first-degree relative was diagnosed before 60. Post-polypectomy intervals depend on number, size, and histology — and on the prep having been adequate in the first place.

Average risk: the menu

USPSTF 2021 — screen ages 45–75; individualize 76–85
TestIntervalNotes
Colonoscopy10 yPrevention + detection; any positive stool test funnels here
FIT1 yNo prep; adherence is the whole game
FIT-DNA (multitarget stool DNA)1–3 yHigher sensitivity than FIT, more false positives

Flexible sigmoidoscopy5 yUncommon in US practice

A positive FIT or stool-DNA test is a colonoscopy indication, not a "repeat the FIT." Delays beyond ~6–9 months after a positive FIT are associated with worse cancer outcomes. Blood-based tests (e.g., cell-free DNA assays, FDA-approved 2024) exist but have lower advanced-adenoma sensitivity — colonoscopy after any positive result.

Family history changes the clock

  • One first-degree relative with CRC or advanced adenoma at any age: start at 40 or 10 years before their diagnosis age, whichever is earlier.
  • FDR diagnosed <60, or two FDRs at any age: colonoscopy every 5 years.
  • FDR diagnosed ≥60: begin at 40; routine intervals thereafter.
  • Red flags for a genetics referral: CRC <50 in the family, ≥10 cumulative adenomas, Lynch-spectrum cancer clustering (endometrial, ovarian, urothelial, gastric).

Hyperplastic polyps <10 mm in the rectosigmoid are not adenomas — normal-exam intervals apply.

Quality: the fine print that makes intervals valid

  • Intervals assume a complete exam to the cecum with adequate prep (Boston Bowel Prep ≥2 per segment). Poor prep → repeat within a year, not "surveillance in 7."
  • Adenoma detection rate is the endoscopist quality metric tied to interval cancer risk — worth knowing as a concept for boards and consults.
  • IBD colitis surveillance is its own pathway (chromoendoscopy/high-definition scopes, starting ~8 years after diagnosis) — don't apply the polyp tables.
  • Stop screening when life expectancy <10 years or after 85; individualize 76–85 by health status and prior screening.
Pearls for the clinic note
  • The most common surveillance error on the wards: scheduling "colonoscopy in 5 years" without reading the pathology. The interval lives in the path report.
  • Document the family history with ages of diagnosis — "father with colon cancer" is incomplete; "at 52" changes the interval.
  • Iron-deficiency anemia in a man or postmenopausal woman is a bidirectional endoscopy referral, not a screening question.
  • Hospitalization is a screening capture opportunity: a discharge order for outpatient FIT/colonoscopy costs one click.
