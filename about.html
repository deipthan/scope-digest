About

Why Scope Digest exists

Internal medicine residents manage a great deal of gastroenterology — GI bleeds at 3 a.m., the cirrhotic with a tense belly, the pancreatitis that won't feed, the colitis that isn't turning the corner — often before a fellow ever sets eyes on the patient. The reference material for that work tends to sit at two extremes: exhaustive society PDFs that are hard to use on call, and quick cards that skip the reasoning. Neither is quite what you need at the bedside.

Scope Digest is a middle path, in two parts. The Digest is twenty focused clinical modules, each built the way you actually manage the patient — resuscitate, risk-stratify, treat, and know exactly when to escalate — with the guideline behind every recommendation named by society and year, and the landmark trials that changed practice pulled into a box you can quote on rounds. The GI Handbook is the comprehensive half: seventy-six core topics across twelve chapters, with diagrams, score calculators, presentation flashcards, and a self-test, for the resident who wants to understand the rotation rather than just survive it.

How pages are built

Every page is grounded in current major society guidelines — ACG, AASLD, AGA, ASGE, IDSA/SHEA, USPSTF and the Multi-Society Task Force, Baveno, and the Tokyo Guidelines among them — cross-referenced against the primary trials. Recommendations are stated with their source and year so you can trace them, and each page carries a review date. Where the field is moving quickly (IBD biologics, microbiota therapies, HRS-AKI), that's flagged rather than smoothed over.

The two-color system is a small nod to endoscopy: teal, the narrow-band-imaging tone, marks evidence and structure; rose, the color of mucosa under white light, is reserved for the moments that mean call GI now. Nothing on the site is decorative for its own sake.

Founder & editor

Deipthan Prabakar, MD is an internal medicine resident at Texas Tech University Health Sciences Center El Paso with a research focus in gastroenterology and endoscopy. His scholarly work includes meta-analyses in endoscopy safety and quality — including a 15-trial meta-analysis of high-flow nasal cannula during upper GI endoscopy recognized with an ACG Presidential Poster Award (2025) — along with work in IBD therapeutics and ctDNA-guided treatment in colorectal cancer.

Scope Digest began as a WhatsApp group he started during residency for co-residents interested in GI. It grew to fifteen residents and two attendings sharing clinical questions, updates in the field, interesting cases, images, and research opportunities. Eventually the group needed an organized place to keep what it was learning, and that became this site: the Digest for the call, and the Handbook for the rotation. Every page is written and reviewed by him; feedback from faculty and colleagues shapes revisions.

The important caveat

This is an educational resource, not medical advice. It does not replace clinical judgment, your institution's protocols, pharmacist verification of doses, or the primary guidelines themselves. Medicine changes; a page reviewed today can be out of date tomorrow. Verify before you act, and when in doubt, ask your seniors and consultants.

Corrections and suggestions are welcome — accuracy matters more than being finished.
