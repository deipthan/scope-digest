Acute GI · Infection

Clostridioides difficile infection

PRIMARY SOURCES: IDSA/SHEA Focused Update (2021) · ACG Guideline (Kelly et al., 2021) · AGA Guideline on fecal microbiota therapies (2024)

GI HandbookSmall Bowel, Colon & Diarrhea · Clostridioides difficile infection→

Bottom line

Test only patients with unexplained, new diarrhea (≥3 unformed stools/24 h) — not formed stools, not "test of cure." First episode: fidaxomicin preferred, oral vancomycin acceptable. Fulminant disease: vancomycin 500 mg PO QID + IV metronidazole, surgery consulted early. Recurrences get a strategy, not a rerun: tapered-pulsed vancomycin or fidaxomicin, bezlotoxumab, and microbiota-based therapy after the second recurrence.

Escalate now
  • Fulminant CDI: hypotension/shock, ileus, or megacolon → maximal medical therapy + early surgical consult (total colectomy or diverting loop ileostomy with lavage)
  • WBC ≥15k or creatinine >1.5 mg/dL = severe disease — don't undertreat
  • Rising lactate or distension on serial exams in a patient who "can't have diarrhea" (ileus) — CDI can be stool-silent when fulminant

Test stewardship first

  • Test only unexplained new diarrhea; laxatives within 48 h invalidate the question.
  • Know your assay: a positive NAAT alone can represent colonization; two-step algorithms (NAAT + toxin EIA) help distinguish. Toxin-positive = treat; NAAT-positive/toxin-negative = clinical judgment.
  • No repeat testing within 7 days and no test of cure — PCR positivity persists after clinical cure.

Treat the episode

Treatment by episode and severity (IDSA 2021 / ACG 2021)
ScenarioRegimen
First episode (non-severe or severe)Fidaxomicin 200 mg BID × 10 d (preferred) or vancomycin 125 mg PO QID × 10 d
FulminantVancomycin 500 mg PO/NG QID + metronidazole 500 mg IV q8h; add rectal vancomycin if ileus; surgery early
First recurrenceFidaxomicin (standard or extended-pulsed), or tapered-and-pulsed vancomycin; consider bezlotoxumab if high recurrence risk
Second or later recurrenceAs above plus microbiota restoration: FMT or approved live biotherapeutics — rectal RBL (Rebyota, 2022) or oral SER-109 (Vowst, 2023)
  • Metronidazole monotherapy is no longer a first-line adult regimen.
  • Stop the inciting antibiotic when possible — the single most effective intervention.

The details that change outcomes

  • If systemic antibiotics must continue, extend CDI therapy through the course plus ~1 week ("secondary prophylaxis" with low-dose vancomycin is commonly practiced, though evidence is observational).
  • Review PPIs — deprescribe if there's no indication.
  • Anti-motility agents: acceptable only once effective CDI therapy is on board; avoid in severe/fulminant disease.
  • Contact precautions, soap-and-water hand hygiene (alcohol doesn't kill spores), private room.
  • CDI in IBD: test every flare; it doubles colectomy risk — treat concurrently with flare therapy.
Pearls for the consult note
  • "NAAT+, toxin−, formed stools, on lactulose" is colonization until proven otherwise — document the reasoning, not just the result.
  • Severity is labs, not stool frequency: WBC ≥15k or Cr >1.5 defines severe.
  • The fulminant patient often stops stooling — falling stool output with rising WBC is an alarm, not improvement.
  • Ask about CDI at medication reconciliation: a recurrence history changes today's antibiotic choices everywhere else in the chart.

Landmark evidence

Trial / studyYearOne-line verdict
Louie (NEJM)2011Fidaxomicin vs vancomycin: similar cure, fewer recurrences.
MODIFY I/II (NEJM)2017Bezlotoxumab reduced recurrent CDI.
van Nood (NEJM)2013Duodenal FMT dramatically outperformed vancomycin for recurrent CDI.
PUNCH CD3 / ECOSPOR III2022–23Live biotherapeutics (RBL, SER-109) reduced recurrence — first FDA-approved microbiota therapies.
