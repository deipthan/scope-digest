Acute GI · Bleeding · Portal hypertension

Variceal bleeding & portal hypertension

PRIMARY SOURCES: Baveno VII Consensus (2022) · AASLD Portal Hypertension Guidance (2024) · García-Pagán NEJM 2010

GI HandbookLiver & Cirrhosis · Portal hypertension and varices→

Bottom line

In a cirrhotic with hematemesis: antibiotics and a vasoactive agent before the scope, restrictive transfusion (Hgb <7, target 7–9 — over-transfusion raises portal pressure), endoscopy within 12 hours with band ligation, then decide who needs pre-emptive TIPS within 72 hours. Afterward: NSBB (carvedilol preferred) plus serial banding.

Call GI now
  • Any suspected variceal bleed — the 12-hour endoscopy clock is already running
  • Uncontrolled bleeding despite octreotide and resuscitation → balloon tamponade or self-expanding esophageal stent as a bridge, and involve IR early for rescue TIPS
  • Child-Pugh C (10–13) or Child-Pugh B with active bleeding at endoscopy → candidate for pre-emptive TIPS within 72 h

Before the scope — the three drugs that save lives

  • Antibiotics: ceftriaxone 1 g IV daily (up to 7 days). Prophylaxis reduces infection, rebleeding, and mortality — the easiest mortality benefit you'll ever order.
  • Vasoactive agent: octreotide 50 mcg IV bolus → 50 mcg/h for 2–5 days (terlipressin where available).
  • PPI until the source is confirmed; stop if purely variceal.
  • Transfuse at Hgb <7. Liberal transfusion in Villanueva 2013 was particularly harmful in cirrhosis — volume raises portal pressure and rebleeding.
  • Airway protection before endoscopy if ongoing hematemesis or grade III–IV encephalopathy. Consider erythromycin for visualization.

Endoscopy within 12 hours

Esophageal varices: band ligation. Gastric varices (IGV1/GOV2): cyanoacrylate injection or endoscopic ultrasound–guided coiling where available; TIPS is often the definitive answer. Failure to control bleeding → tamponade/stent as a bridge to rescue TIPS.

The pre-emptive TIPS decision (day 0–3)

García-Pagán (NEJM 2010) and Baveno VII: patients at high risk of treatment failure — Child-Pugh C (10–13), or Child-Pugh B >7 with active bleeding at endoscopy — benefit from TIPS within 72 h of the index bleed, with improved survival. This decision belongs in your day-1 assessment, not as an afterthought after rebleeding.

Secondary prophylaxis — nobody leaves without a plan

  • NSBB + serial band ligation until eradication, then surveillance endoscopy.
  • Carvedilol (start 6.25 mg daily) is the preferred NSBB in Baveno VII — greater portal pressure reduction than propranolol/nadolol.
  • Hold or reduce NSBB in refractory ascites with SBP <90, Na <130, or AKI — and revisit when hemodynamics recover.

Zoom out: who needed prophylaxis before they ever bled?

Baveno VII reframed the question from "screen for varices" to "treat clinically significant portal hypertension (CSPH)." Liver stiffness ≥25 kPa suffices for CSPH; these patients benefit from carvedilol to prevent decompensation, not just bleeding. Screening endoscopy can be avoided when stiffness <20 kPa and platelets >150k (Baveno criteria).

Pearls for the consult note
  • Write the antibiotic and octreotide orders before you call GI — it's the first thing the fellow will ask.
  • Document Child-Pugh on day 1: it decides pre-emptive TIPS eligibility while the window is still open.
  • Rebleeding within 5 days ≠ failure of your plan; it's the trigger for rescue TIPS.
  • An "upper GI bleed, r/o varices" in an unknown patient: liver stiffness, platelets, and exam stigmata sort the pretest probability quickly.

Landmark evidence

Trial / studyYearOne-line verdict
García-Pagán (NEJM)2010Early (pre-emptive) TIPS in high-risk variceal bleeding improved survival.
Villanueva (NEJM)2013Restrictive transfusion improved outcomes, most markedly in cirrhosis.
Bernard meta-analysis1999Antibiotic prophylaxis in cirrhotic GI bleeding reduces infection and mortality.
PREDESCI (Lancet)2019NSBB in compensated CSPH reduced decompensation — the trial behind treating CSPH itself.
