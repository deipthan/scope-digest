Peptic ulcer disease & H. pylori

PRIMARY SOURCES: ACG Clinical Guideline: Treatment of Helicobacter pylori Infection (Chey et al., 2017) · Maastricht VI/Florence consensus (2022)

Bottom line

Every ulcer gets a cause: test for H. pylori and ask about NSAIDs. Because clarithromycin resistance is now widespread, empiric bismuth quadruple therapy for 14 days is the preferred first-line regimen in most of the US. Heal the ulcer with a PPI, and — this is the step residents forget — confirm eradication at least 4 weeks after therapy, off PPI for 1–2 weeks, with a urea breath test or stool antigen.

Escalate now
  • Active bleeding (hematemesis, melena, hemodynamic change) → resuscitate and treat as upper GI bleed; see that pathway
  • Sudden severe epigastric pain with a rigid abdomen or free air on imaging → perforation, surgical emergency
  • Persistent vomiting with succussion splash / retained food → gastric outlet obstruction

Establish the diagnosis and the cause

  • Endoscopy diagnoses the ulcer; biopsy every gastric ulcer to exclude malignancy and confirm healing on follow-up.
  • Test for H. pylori in all PUD. Biopsy-based testing during EGD, or non-invasive urea breath test / stool antigen. Serology cannot distinguish active from past infection.
  • Recent PPIs, bismuth, or antibiotics cause false-negative active tests — interpret accordingly.

Eradicate H. pylori

First-line regimens (14 days preferred; ACG 2017 / Maastricht VI)
RegimenComponents
Bismuth quadruple (preferred)PPI BID + bismuth subsalicylate/subcitrate QID + tetracycline 500 mg QID + metronidazole 500 mg TID–QID
Clarithromycin triplePPI BID + clarithromycin 500 mg BID + amoxicillin 1 g BID — only where resistance <15% and no prior macrolide exposure
Rifabutin triple (Talicia)Omeprazole + amoxicillin + rifabutin — packaged salvage/first-line option
  • Ask about prior macrolide use for any indication — one course is enough to select resistance and doom clarithromycin triple therapy.
  • Penicillin allergy: bismuth quadruple avoids amoxicillin and is the natural choice.

Heal, confirm, and prevent recurrence

  • Continue a PPI to heal the ulcer (4–8 weeks; longer for large or complicated ulcers).
  • Confirm eradication with UBT or stool antigen ≥4 weeks after antibiotics and ≥1–2 weeks off PPI. Serology is useless for this.
  • Stop the offending NSAID. If NSAIDs are unavoidable: lowest effective dose, add a PPI, consider a COX-2–selective agent, and eradicate H. pylori before long-term NSAID/aspirin use.
  • Idiopathic (H. pylori–negative, NSAID-negative) ulcers exist and carry higher rebleeding risk — they often warrant indefinite PPI.
Pearls for the note
  • "H. pylori treated" without documented confirmation is an incomplete plan — write the confirmation test and its timing.
  • A non-healing gastric ulcer on repeat EGD is cancer until proven otherwise — re-biopsy.
  • Duodenal ulcers are almost always H. pylori or NSAIDs; a duodenal ulcer negative for both should prompt a gastrinoma thought (Zollinger-Ellison) if multiple/refractory/distal.
  • Hold the PPI before eradication testing, not before you send it — false negatives are the most common preventable error here.
